Roots & Routes: dancing between where we come from and where we are going.

01 / Roots

Contact Improvisation as a practice of grounding: weight, touch, listening and trust. We return to the roots of the form, to its first questions and the knowledge passed on from body to body, and ask what these roots mean in our own practice today.

02 / Routs

Every dancer arrives by a different route: across countries, languages, histories and borders. The festival opens space for these journeys to meet, from the studio floor to vertical dance off the ground, and for conversations about identity, body politics and what it means to move together in a changing world.

LAB/WORKSHOP

Somatic Harmony ~ Cultivating Balance

A movement laboratory exploring one pathway into Contact Improvisation through a holistic approach to the body. Breath, somatic awareness and mindful movement invite us into contact with ourselves: listening to sensation, emotion and impulse and allowing honest expression to become dance.

From this connection, we open towards contact with other bodies, exploring how our inner experience meets touch, shared weight and movement. Improvisation becomes both a vulnerable, authentic way of expressing ourselves and a tool for listening and responding within contact. Rooted in self-awareness, we discover new roads into a shared dance.

WORKSHOP

Contact Kids or Little Curiosity ~ Connected Play for Children and Adults

A movement laboratory for children and adults, exploring one possible road into Contact Improvisation through curiosity, play and childlike discovery. Movement games, storytelling and imagination invite us to follow what catches our attention, listen through touch and discover ways of moving together.

We explore rolling, leaning, sharing weight and responding to one another, with room for each person’s boundaries and choices. Children and adults become partners in discovery, allowing spontaneous encounters to become a shared dance.

Artist/Performer/Teacher

Olek Wojcik

“The world is our playground, and each interaction is an opportunity to learn something new.”

Olek is a freelance physical artist and explorer, passionate about weaving diverse disciplines into tools for authentic expression and storytelling. Drawing inspiration from Parkour, Contact Improvisation, Acro dance, fire performance, Capoeira, and street theater, he creates dynamic narratives that transcend boundaries of movement and emotion.

As both a performer and facilitator, Olek thrives on discovering new ways to connect body, mind, and soul through active play and creative exploration. His work invites others to embrace their full potential, cultivating presence and a deeper understanding of themselves through the art of movement and its limitless possibilities.

Olek's philosophy is to bring movement back to the outside environment as play. Growing these essential skills on the streets and making it accessible to everyone, to support our connection and wellbeing in our society with the vision of building a movement culture as an ordinary part of our existence.
LABORATORY

The Art of Taking Risk ~ A Laboratory of respons-ability

A movement laboratory exploring risk both as a root of Contact Improvisation and a road towards new possibilities.

Through somatic preparation and practical explorations of balance, momentum, falling and flying, Olek invite us to investigate how we meet uncertainty and choose how to respond.

We practise assessing risk through attention to our own readiness, our partners and the space: recognising limits, communicating boundaries and finding support. Moving between softness and dynamic play, we explore how to take risks with greater awareness and care, allowing challenge to expand our choices within a shared dance.

Contact Improvisation LABORATORY

Jam Sweet/Sour

How to make CI jamming an interesting, enjoyable practice? This question is especially
relevant for people who are new to this form, but it also comes up for long-term CI dancers.
I jam regularly for more than 25 years by now. I still enjoy it and I still learn from it. In this
intensive lab I want to share some strategies and tools around how to enter a jam, how to
feel involved, find a focus, and shift focus during jamming.
We’ll address and explore issues around dancing, watching, resting, studying, choice
making, caring, fear, self-protection, finding and dropping energy, connection, and artistic
pleasure while jamming. We will spend time not only on jam pleasure strategies and jam
survival strategies but also on some basic technical issues like rolling, centre to centre work,
gradually giving weight to a partner and (importantly) taking weight off again. Overall, we

will use the lab to support each other in expanding our repertoires as jammers and CI
dancers. Let’s improve our virtuosity in creating joyful dances together.

LECTURE/TALK

"Dancing Aging"

Susanne will present some aspects of her doctoral dissertation “Dancing Age(ing)”, which
explores the special potential of contemporary dance and particularly contact improvisation
to embody age and ageing differently – to question and critique stereotypical and (self-)
discriminatory notions of getting old and staying young. Ageing is a lifelong process, and it is
a difference we are ALL growing into (if we are lucky). In other words: this is not a talk for
50+ dancers. It is a joyful call to rethink our relationship with ourselves and with one
another, and a very interesting topic to reflect on at any stage of one’s dancing life! There
will be time for group reflection and discussion.

Filmaker

Serhii Semichev

Serhii Semichev is a dancer, improviser, performer, Contact Improvisation teacher, photographer and filmmaker. Born in Snezhnoe, in Ukraine's Donetsk region, he trained from childhood in acrobatics, gymnastics and circus school, and later qualified as a massage therapist.

He began teaching Contact Improvisation in Donetsk in 2013 and went on to help build the CI community in Kyiv, where he taught, organised events and ran a Contact Improvisation research laboratory until 2022. Since then he has taught and performed at festivals and conferences in Ukraine, Moldova, Portugal, Sweden, Germany, Austria and China, and co-organised the Dance Pavilion festival of Contact Improvisation and dance film.

Camera and dance have grown side by side in his work. He creates dance films, documents festivals as a photographer and videographer, and worked as videographer and editor on the Austrian project Code of Dance, including the making of the dance film Drift. On stage he has appeared in The Silence of the Mass (Motion Mode Dance Theatre, Innsbruck), which he also choreographed, and in the Freiburg project Labormanifest. In 2026 he organised and performed in the open stage Shape It at Cargo Theater Freiburg.

Based in Freiburg, Germany, he is currently completing the documentary The Path, a film of conversations with Contact Improvisation teachers from different countries.
Film Showcase

​"The Path"

​Film duration: approx. 60 minutes.
​We have inherited a small amount of documentary video footage about the origins and early development of Contact Improvisation.
​Generations of dancers and teachers have already changed since the practice began, yet many continue walking the chosen path. Some have been on this path from the very beginning. Where are we going? In what direction is the practice evolving?
​This film brings together footage showcasing contemporary Contact Improvisation, alongside interview fragments with various Contact Improvisation teachers from different countries, filmed in 2026.
​All teachers were asked the same questions:
​Do you remember your first encounter with Contact Improvisation? What did you find interesting in this practice at the beginning of your journey? What touched you?
​Has your attitude toward Contact Improvisation changed after many years of practicing, dancing, and teaching? If so, how? What does Contact Improvisation mean to you today?
​This film does not provide a definitive definition of what Contact Improvisation is today, but it offers many stories and opinions from people who have dedicated a part of their lives to this practice.

Outdoor vertical dance laboratory

Vertical Dance in the Tyrolean Nature

A somatic exploration of the body in the vertical. Suspended on the rope, we work with weight, orientation and the sensation of moving between earth and air. The class combines improvisation, vertical dance technique and partnering on the wall, always listening to how the body organizes itself when the floor becomes a wall.

Like in 2026, we are planning to hold it outdoors on the rocks of Tyrol or at other outdoor locations, weather permitting. Details are still being finalized.

The Wild One

Venue: Theatre Praesent, Innsbruck · Artists: Olek, Laura and Teya

The Wild One: The Adventures of Playful Curiosity in the Realms of the Timeless Present Moment
The Wild One is a family acro-theatre performance that invites audiences to awaken their senses, explore their potential, and rediscover the unfiltered joy of being present. Through improvisation and play, performers and spectators embark on a shared journey where imagination and reality intertwine, breaking down boundaries and opening pathways to creativity and expression. Each moment unfolds as a response to the living energy of the participants, creating a dynamic and immersive experience.
At its heart, the performance celebrates childlike curiosity—the freedom to explore, feel, and create. By blurring the line between what is real and imagined, The Wild One dares to ask: What hidden wishes and sparks of creativity lie within you? Through collaboration and mutual discovery, the show becomes a vibrant canvas of experimentation, gently inspiring audiences to embrace their inner playfulness, reawaken their creativity, and rediscover the aliveness that comes from being fully present in each moment.
